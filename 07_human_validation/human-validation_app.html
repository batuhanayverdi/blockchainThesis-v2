How to review

You will read short blog posts. In each post, the sentence(s) we want you to check are highlighted in yellow. Judge the highlighted statement(s) together.

  1. Read the post and the highlighted claim(s).
  2. Open Google in another tab and search for the claim. Check the news as well, the way you would if you were verifying it yourself.
  3. Decide how accurate the highlighted claim(s) are, taken together, and choose one label.
  4. An error does not by itself make a claim False. If the claim still carries a real element of truth but leaves a misleading impression, it is Mostly false. Use False when the claim is simply not accurate.
  5. Choose Unverified or cannot determine only when, after searching, you still cannot tell whether the claim is true or false, because reliable evidence is missing or sources genuinely conflict. Do not use it for a claim that is part true and part false; that is Mostly false or False.
  6. Tick the checkbox if the highlighted texts are checkable factual claims (statements that could be true or false), rather than opinions or questions.

What the labels mean

  • True: accurate, with nothing significant missing.
  • Mostly true: accurate but needs clarification or additional information.
  • Mostly false: contains an element of truth but ignores critical facts that would give a different impression.
  • False: not accurate.
  • Pants on fire: not accurate, and additionally makes an absurd or egregious claim with no basis in fact.
  • Unverified or cannot determine: after searching, the evidence is missing or genuinely conflicting, so you cannot tell.
